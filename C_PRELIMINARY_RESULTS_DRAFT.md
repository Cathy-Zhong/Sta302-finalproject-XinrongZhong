# Preliminary Results

We fitted a multiple linear regression using 72,973 completed bookings. This preliminary model included 14 predictors and a lead-time-by-hotel interaction term, allowing the association between lead time and mean ADR to differ between the two hotels.

Our preliminary multiple linear regression estimates for ADR, as well as the standard errors and 95% confidence intervals, can be seen in the model table.

For the City Hotel, one additional day of lead time was associated with a 0.0944-euro decrease in mean ADR (95% CI: -0.0979, -0.0909), holding the other predictors fixed. The lead-time-by-hotel interaction estimate was 0.0551 euros (95% CI: 0.0498, 0.0604), giving a Resort Hotel slope of -0.0393 euros per day. Longer lead times were associated with lower ADR at both hotels, but the negative association was weaker at the Resort Hotel. The interaction confidence interval did not contain zero, providing evidence that the slopes differed between the hotels. This difference was consistent with Abrate et al. (2012), who found that inter-temporal hotel pricing varies with hotel and customer characteristics. The adjusted R-squared was 0.5823. 

Then considering the pairwise plots of the numerical predictors, the observed-versus-fitted plot shows systematic departures from the reference line. The pairwise plots do not show strong curved relationships among the numerical predictors, although several count predictors are concentrated at small values. Therefore, the conditional mean response condition is questionable, while the conditional mean predictor condition appears reasonable for the numerical predictors.

The linearity and constant variance assumptions are questionable due to occurance of systematic patterns and changing spread in the residuals-fitted and residuals-leadtime plots. The remaining residual plots are generally centered near zero, although discrete predictors form bands and some categories have different spreads. The Q-Q plot has a significant strong tail pattern, gives the evidence that normality assumption might not be reasonable. Group or repeat bookings may produce related errors, but the data do not contain enough identifying information to verify the uncorrelated-errors assumption.
