# Preliminary Results

We fitted a preliminary multiple linear regression to 72,973 completed bookings. The model included lead time, hotel, arrival month, weekend and weekday nights, adults, children, meal, market segment, customer type, reserved room type, repeat-guest status, special requests, and domestic status, together with the lead-time-by-hotel interaction.

**Table 1.** Preliminary multiple linear regression estimates for ADR, with standard errors and 95% confidence intervals.

At the City Hotel, one additional day of lead time was associated with a 0.0944-euro lower mean ADR (95% CI: -0.0979, -0.0909), holding the other predictors fixed. The interaction estimate was 0.0551 euros (95% CI: 0.0498, 0.0604), giving a Resort Hotel slope of -0.0393 euros per day. Longer lead times were associated with lower ADR at both hotels, but the negative association was weaker at the Resort Hotel. The significant interaction was consistent with Abrate et al. (2012), who found that inter-temporal hotel pricing varies with hotel and customer characteristics. The adjusted R-squared was 0.5823. These estimates describe associations, not causal effects.

**Figure 1.** Residual plots for the preliminary model: (a) residuals versus fitted ADR, (b) residuals in data order, and (c) the normal Q-Q plot.

The residuals-versus-fitted plot shows systematic structure and increasing spread, so the linearity and constant variance assumptions are questionable. The residuals also show long runs above and below zero in data order, making the uncorrelated-errors assumption questionable. The Q-Q plot departs strongly from the line in both tails, so the normality assumption is not reasonable. Following the Part 1 instructions, we identify these violations without correcting them in the preliminary model.
