# Preliminary Results

We fitted a preliminary multiple linear regression using 72,973 completed bookings. The model included 14 predictors and a lead-time-by-hotel interaction. The interaction allowed the association between lead time and mean ADR to differ between the City Hotel and Resort Hotel.

**Table 1.** Preliminary multiple linear regression estimates for ADR, with standard errors and 95% confidence intervals. The reference levels are City Hotel, January, BB meal, Online TA, Transient customer, room type A, non-repeat guest, and international guest.

For the City Hotel, one additional day of lead time was associated with a 0.0944-euro decrease in mean ADR (95% CI: -0.0979, -0.0909), holding the other predictors fixed. The lead-time-by-hotel interaction estimate was 0.0551 euros (95% CI: 0.0498, 0.0604). Therefore, the estimated lead-time slope for the Resort Hotel was -0.0393 euros per day. Longer lead times were associated with lower ADR at both hotels, but the negative association was weaker at the Resort Hotel. The interaction confidence interval did not contain zero, providing evidence that the lead-time slopes differed between the two hotels. This hotel-specific difference was consistent with Abrate et al. (2012), who found that inter-temporal hotel pricing varies with hotel and customer characteristics. The adjusted R-squared was 0.5823. These results describe conditional associations, not causal effects.

**Figure 1.** Residual plots for the preliminary model: (a) residuals versus fitted ADR, (b) residuals versus lead time, and (c) the normal Q-Q plot.

The residuals-versus-fitted and residuals-versus-lead-time plots show systematic patterns and changing spread. Therefore, the linearity and constant variance assumptions are questionable. The normal Q-Q plot shows substantial departures from the reference line in both tails, so the normality assumption is not reasonable. Group bookings or repeat guests may produce related errors, but the available data do not contain enough identifying information to verify the uncorrelated-errors assumption. Following the Part 1 instructions, we identify these violations without correcting them in the preliminary model.
